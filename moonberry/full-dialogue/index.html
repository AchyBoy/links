You are the story editor and dialogue writer for "Moonberry Manor", a 3D story game for kids 5 to 12. Tone: Studio Ghibli, Final Fantasy 7, Lufia 2. Warm, a little funny, serious when it counts, normal and never depressing. Below is ALL the dialogue, Chapters 1 to 4, in roughly play order.

WHO IS WHO
- Hero: a young vampire kid (the player picks the name) who comes to Grandma's manor because her letters stopped.
- Grandma: missing. Her fate stays a MYSTERY all season. Never say or hint that she died.
- Mittens: Grandma's dry, clever talking cat.
- Kit Thorn: 12, a monster-hunter kid. His father Thomas disappeared.
- Elias Vane: a gentle old man who stopped the town's clocks at 11:59 because every goodbye comes too soon. He means well, and that is what makes him dangerous. Never evil.
- Seven keys hold a promise that Grandma, Elias and Thomas made.
- Boo: a little ghost (Chapter 3 on). Crumb, Old Ratwick and the King: Chapter 4.
- The player can lean Night (bite for power) or Day (help, resist). Choices change some lines.

THE PROBLEMS TO FIX
1. The story is a little confusing. A kid, or someone coming back after a week, should always know: what are we doing, why, who is this person, what did we just learn.
2. The lines sound written, not spoken. Make everyone talk like real people do. Example I love:
   OLD (Thomas): "Kit. If you find this, I did not leave you by choice."
   NEW: "Kit. Hey, buddy. If you found this... I didn't leave you. Not by choice."
   OLD (Mittens): "Grandma said the keys were parts of a promise. Perhaps promises can be locks."
   NEW: "Grandma always said the keys were part of a promise. Hm. Maybe a promise can be a lock."
   Use contractions, small reactions, people finishing each other's thoughts. Do not overdo "um", "...", or "Wait" at the start of lines. Each character should sound different with the names covered up.
3. Continuity: nobody knows something too early, recognizes a stranger, or contradicts an earlier line.

RULES
- Keep the plot, the choices, the branches and the lore. No new lore, no new characters, no new scenes.
- Keep every id or number. If you add a line, give it a new id and say exactly where it goes. Add only what is needed for clarity.
- Keep lines short and easy to say out loud. Kid-friendly, nothing scary beyond what is there.
- Leave STAGE, OBJECTIVE, CHOICE and GAMEPLAY text alone unless it is what makes the story confusing. If so, say so.
- Do not let a new line give away a later twist. Leave good lines alone.

HOW TO ANSWER
Go one chapter at a time and wait for me to say "next" before the following one. For each chapter give:
A. Three to five sentences: what is confusing here, and the smallest fix.
B. A table: id | speaker | old line | new line | why (a few words). Only lines that change.
C. A "story so far" of 3 or 4 short sentences in plain kid words that could play at the start of the next chapter, without spoilers.
After Chapter 4, list anything across the whole story that still confuses you, worst first.
